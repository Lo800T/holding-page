What we trace

Following value across borders, structures and ledgers.

We establish what a person or company owns, where it is held, who controls it and how it has moved, and we produce sourced findings that support recovery, enforcement and litigation.

Property & real estate

Residential and commercial property in the UK and abroad, held directly, jointly, through companies or through connected parties, including what was owned before and what has quietly been sold.

Questions we answer

  • What does the subject own now, and what did they own before?
  • Who co-owns it, and is it mortgaged or charged?
  • Has it been sold, transferred or gifted, and where did the proceeds go?

Where we look

Current and historic title registers, charges, gazettes, planning and council records, foreign land indices and notarial records, and specialist local providers in each jurisdiction.

What you get

A property schedule with title evidence, owners and co-owners, charges and a disposal history, each entry linked to its source document.

Companies, trusts & beneficial ownership

Mapping companies, trusts and nominees to the people who actually control them, across multiple jurisdictions and through layered or offshore structures.

Questions we answer

  • Which companies does the subject own or control, directly or through nominees?
  • Who is the real beneficial owner behind the structure?
  • Which vehicle actually holds the value?

Where we look

Corporate registries in more than 100 countries, beneficial ownership and overseas-entity registers, filings and accounts, court and insolvency records, archived and historic records.

What you get

An ownership map from subject to asset, with officers, shareholders, nominees and related parties, and every link sourced.

Digital assets

On-chain tracing is only half the job. The value is in the bridge: turning wallets and transaction flows into real people, companies and recoverable assets.

Questions we answer

  • Where did the funds go, across wallets, chains and mixers?
  • Which exchange or service provider can be asked, or ordered, to disclose?
  • Who is the real person behind the wallet?

Where we look

Blockchain analytics, wallet clustering and exchange attribution, then off-chain evidence: companies, domains, social footprints, property and people that link a wallet to an identity.

What you get

Flow tracing, wallet attribution and the off-ramp, linked to real-world identities and assets, to support disclosure applications and freezing relief.

Legal context: the Property (Digital Assets etc) Act 2025, which received Royal Assent on 2 December 2025, confirms that digital assets can attract personal property rights under the law of England and Wales.

High-value & lifestyle assets

Yachts, aircraft, prestige vehicles, art and other luxury assets, which are rarely held in the subject's own name.

Questions we answer

  • Does the subject own, or simply use, a vessel, aircraft or collection?
  • Which company, trust or relative holds it?
  • Does the lifestyle match the means the subject has declared?

Where we look

Aircraft and ship registers, movement histories, vehicle and marine records, auction and dealer records, and the subject's lifestyle footprint online.

What you get

An asset list with registration evidence, the holding structure and the people connected to it.

Disposals & dissipation

Tracing the sale or transfer of property and other assets, and the related parties and corporate vehicles the value passes through.

Questions we answer

  • Has the subject moved assets out of reach, before or after a claim?
  • Who received them: a spouse, a partner, a relative, a “friendly” company?
  • What did the disposal realise, and where did the money go next?

Where we look

Historic registry snapshots, title and charge histories, company filings, connected-party analysis and dated open-source evidence.

What you get

A chronology of disposals and the parties the value passed through: the evidence behind freezing applications and claims against transactions that defraud creditors.

Fraud & scam proceeds

Following the proceeds of fraud, scam networks and money laundering to the entities, individuals and assets at the end of the chain.

Questions we answer

  • Where did the victims' money go?
  • Who really controls the receiving companies?
  • Which assets can be frozen, forfeited or claimed?

Where we look

On-chain analysis, shell-company clustering, nominee-officer patterns, cross-border beneficial ownership and sanctions and enforcement records.

What you get

A network map with recovery targets, prepared to work alongside legal teams and victims' advisers.

People & debtors

Confirmed address traces for service of process, judgment enforcement and debt recovery, in the UK and internationally.

Questions we answer

  • Where is the subject now?
  • Are we certain we have the right person?
  • What links them to other people, companies and addresses?

Where we look

Specialist databases, property and electoral records and open sources, with identity confirmed against at least three independent identifiers.

What you get

A confirmed address report, suitable for service, with the evidence of identity set out.
